Type IL Cement & Concrete Index

A living index of standards, research, guidance, field experience and news on Type IL portland-limestone cement (ASTM C595 / AASHTO M 240, 5-15% limestone) and concrete made with it. Updated daily.

Type IL is a binary blended cement with more than 5% and up to 15% limestone, designed for 1:1 replacement of ASTM C150 Type I/II with similar 28-day strength and about 8-10% lower carbon footprint. It is accepted by essentially all state DOTs and became the dominant U.S. cement by 2024. The main practical issues are finishing and setting behavior in flatwork, source-to-source variability, sulfate (thaumasite) exposure, and the 2026 C595 revisions that broaden blended cement composition.

Standards and specifications

  • ASTM C595 / AASHTO M 240 (harmonized): Type IL(xx), xx = limestone %; >5% to 15%. Same set and strength limits as portland cement. Suffixes (MS), (HS), (HE), (A).
  • vs ASTM C150 / M 85: Portland cement allows up to 5% limestone since 2004 (M 85 2007). ACI 225.2 advises listing C595 IL beside C150 Type I, not raising cement content.
  • Model specs: AIA MasterSpec 03 30 00 and FAA P-501 (EB 106) include Type IL.
  • 2026 revisions: C595-26 adds Type IC (ASTM only), more limestone in IP/IS; IT needs two SCMs.
  • ASTM work item WK94958 is the revision that bases sulfate-resistant Type IL designations on ASTM C452 testing (R74).

State DOT acceptance

  • Broad acceptance: PCA reports acceptance by 50 states by Feb 2024.
  • Limestone limits: Nov 2024 survey: only Idaho capped limestone below 15% (12.5%).
  • IL(HE) emerging: Ohio has a spec section, NYSDOT approved one product, others trialing as Type III replacement.
  • Approved lists: TxDOT Item 421, Caltrans authorized list (M 240), MnDOT certified sources.
  • Tighter caps returning: INDOT RSP 901-M-069 (effective Dec 2025) limits limestone in Type IL to 10% by mass of the finished cement, including production tolerance.
  • NYSDOT's Sept 2026 specs accept Type IL under AASHTO M 240 but require the portland cement in any blend to be Type II or II(MH) (R69); INDOT's 2026 book lists Type IL under M 240 (R70).

Carbon, GWP and EPDs

  • About 8% lower GWP: Industry EPDs: PLC (10.8% limestone) is 8.2% lower than portland cement; FHWA cites 8.3%; ACI up to 10%.
  • Grinding: Extra grinding energy is outweighed by clinker reduction.
  • New PCR (Oct 2026): ACA is surveying U.S. producers for updated industry-average EPDs, including portland-limestone cement.
  • Plant-specific Type IL EPDs keep appearing, e.g. Amrize Devils Slide IL(10), issued Feb 2026 (R68).

Fresh properties and admixtures

  • Higher fineness: Can raise water demand about 1-3 gal/yd3 and reduce bleeding.
  • Admixtures: Re-tune dosages; avoid over-dosing retarding water reducers; verify air entrainment per source.
  • Setting: Reports of unpredictable or differential set and top-down crusting in flatwork.

Strength, curing, cold weather

  • Similar 28-day strength: Some field reports of lower early strength and more weather sensitivity.
  • Type IL(HE): High-early PLC replacing Type III in precast and cold weather.
  • Cold weather: Standard ACI 306 practice applies; protect until about 500 psi.

Durability

  • Sulfate and thaumasite: PLC alone is more susceptible to thaumasite attack at low temperature; SCMs at sulfate-resisting levels mitigate. Use IL(MS)/IL(HS) or C1012 testing.
  • Freeze-thaw and scaling: Air, low w/cm, finishing and curing govern; scaling of IL concrete is debated (ACI Point of View).
  • ASR, chloride: Handled with SCMs as for portland cement; Purdue found PLC-only chloride diffusion up to 30% higher, much lower with fly ash.
  • Abrasion and carbonation: 2026 Temple study: comparable abrasion when properly proportioned and finished.
  • Sulfate designation changed in C595-26 / M 240-26: Type IL (MS)/(HS) now based mainly on ASTM C452 14-day expansion (MS at 0.050% max) rather than C1012 (Giannini, CI Oct 2026).
  • 2026 papers add caution for aggressive exposures: PLC paste lost far more mass than OPC in citric acid (R66), limestone concrete deteriorated most under combined sulfate and chloride attack while fly ash held up (R67), and a critical review argues durability, not clinker cut alone, should decide whether limestone-rich binders are truly low-carbon (R65).

Use with SCMs

  • Synergy: Limestone with aluminous SCMs (fly ash, slag) forms carboaluminates, refining pores.
  • Ternary mixes: Common in U.S. practice; needed for severe sulfate exposure.

  • Oklahoma I-40 case study: Performance comparable to Type I with no reported mixing, placing or finishing differences.

Precast, floors, bridges

  • Precast and prestressed: PCI Journal 2025 and USDOT research: IL usable in girders; prestress loss and transfer length favorable.
  • Industrial floors: Comparable abrasion with good finishing and curing.
  • Tilt-up: Bond breaker compatibility concerns raised with C595-26 compositions.
  • Shotcrete: ACI Materials Journal 2026 study found PLC wet-mix shotcrete needs more alkali-free accelerator for early strength, with similar 7- and 28-day strength and fiber toughness to portland cement.
  • 3D printing: Early studies use Type IL / GULb binders with cellulose nanomaterials or quarry fines to meet printability.

Mix design guidance

  • 1:1 replacement: Keep cement content; run trial batches and verify fresh and hardened properties.
  • Adjust: Water within w/cm limit, admixture type and dosage, aggregate grading.

Field issues and finishing

  • Symptoms: Crusting, differential set, crazing, delamination, plastic shrinkage cracking, low breaks.
  • Remedies: Control set (accelerators), evaporation retarder or fogging at 0.05-0.08 lb/ft2/hr, flat first trowel, no early sealing, no over-troweling.
  • A March 2026 two-hour WACA training deck covers placing and finishing Type IL concrete (R71).

Supply, producers, adoption

  • Market share: Blended cement about 5% of U.S. market in 2021; about 65 Mt vs 35 Mt portland in 2025 (ACA).
  • Producers: Examples seen: Amrize, Cemex, Heidelberg, GCC, St. Marys/Votorantim, Illinois Cement.
  • Variability: Switching IL sources is a recognized concern.

Canada and Europe

  • CSA A3001 GUL: Up to 15% limestone since 2008; same requirements as GU; Blaine typically about 100 m2/kg higher.
  • EN 197-1 CEM II/A-L, B-L: In use in Europe since the 1960s-2000s; may be lower strength class than CEM I, unlike U.S./Canadian equivalence.
  • GCCA: Treats limestone as a clinker substitute with synergies with other SCMs; flags clay and organic contamination as frost-durability risks.

Microcracking and the interfacial transition zone

  • The ITZ (about 10-50 µm around aggregate) is porous and rich in calcium hydroxide, and microcracks start there first (Mehta & Monteiro; Girboveanu 2021).
  • Drying shrinkage of the paste is restrained by stiff aggregate, which causes microcracks around aggregates. Crack amount depends on aggregate size, volume fraction and stiffness mismatch (Grassl 2010; Li 2020; Bisschop 2002).
  • Type IL / PLC: the Purdue JTRP 2025 report found PLC concretes form thinner ITZs than OPC (filler effect), but water reducers raised ITZ porosity and CH build-up in one PLC, weakening it.
  • Limestone filler: about 5% densifies the ITZ while 10% or more can raise ITZ porosity, and calcite collects at the interface (Ghent; Kotlanova 2021/2022). Limestone surfaces nucleate C-S-H well (Ouyang 2017).
  • Limestone aggregate can bond chemically with paste at later ages (Mehta & Monteiro). Larger limestone grains showed fewer ITZ microcracks (Golewski 2025).
  • Methods: micro-CT/3D imaging (Mac 2021), acoustic emission separates aggregate cracks from paste/ITZ cracks (Farnam 2015), DIC at the ITZ (Zhang 2023), fluorescence microscopy for freeze-thaw (Li 2015).

Key issues

  • Finishing and set in slabs: Industry guidance exists but standards for finishing aids are minimal.
  • Source-to-source variability: Requalify mixes when cement source changes.
  • Sulfate and low-temperature exposure: Thaumasite risk without adequate SCMs.
  • Deicer scaling: Open debate on IL concrete scaling.
  • C595-26 Type IC and higher limestone IP/IS: Cement type label conveys less about composition.
  • Early strength: IL(HE) acceptance still emerging at DOTs.
  • State limestone caps: Some DOTs (Indiana at 10%) cap IL limestone below the 15% C595 maximum, so a national IL product may not qualify everywhere.
  • Aggressive chemical exposure: New 2026 studies show PLC/limestone systems can underperform OPC or fly ash mixes in acid and combined sulfate-chloride exposure, so SCMs and exposure-specific testing matter.
  • ITZ in PLC: PLC tends to form a thinner ITZ, but admixture interactions and filler levels above about 10% can make it more porous. Direct imaging of microcracks in Type IL vs OPC concrete is still scarce.

Research gaps

  • Field performance data: Lab-to-field correlation and performance-based specs still developing.
  • Partly covered now: Shotcrete (one 2026 ACI study) and 3D printing (two lab studies).
  • Partly covered now: SCC (one 2018 dissertation) and mass concrete heat (one 2017 field study).
  • Not yet covered by found sources: cost data specific to Type IL from a reliable source; primary ACI 318/301 code text; EN 197-1 official text.
  • Microcracking: no direct study found that images ITZ microcracks (SEM, CT, AE or DIC) in commercial Type IL vs OPC concrete, separates limestone aggregate from limestone filler effects in PLC, or applies DIC to PLC. No NIST or FHWA report specific to ITZ cracking in PLC was found.
