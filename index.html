Standards and specifications

Type IL collection. These points are not general rules for every cement or concrete.

  • ASTM C595 / AASHTO M 240 (harmonized): Type IL(xx), xx = limestone %; >5% to 15%. Same set and strength limits as portland cement. Suffixes (MS), (HS), (HE), (A).
  • vs ASTM C150 / M 85: Portland cement allows up to 5% limestone since 2004 (M 85 2007). ACI 225.2 advises listing C595 IL beside C150 Type I, not raising cement content.
  • Model specs: AIA MasterSpec 03 30 00 and FAA P-501 (EB 106) include Type IL.
  • 2026 revisions: C595-26 adds Type IC (ASTM only), more limestone in IP/IS; IT needs two SCMs.
  • ASTM work item WK94958 is the revision that bases sulfate-resistant Type IL designations on ASTM C452 testing (R74).
  • NRMCA's 2026 specification guide (R98) recommends allowing ASTM C595 Type IL rather than mandating a cement type, and notes IL can be qualified as MS or HS.
  • FHWA-HRT-25-116 (R125, Sept 2025) surveyed 18 commercial PLCs from 13 plants in 9 states: CaCO3 averaged ~10% (estimated limestone ~7–18%); Blaine often poorly correlates with particle-size/reactivity; sulfate optimization is critical for early hydration.
  • ACA hub (R138) states ACI 318 and ACI 301 include PLCs/blended cements, along with ASTM C94, ICC, FAA and AIA MasterSpec — primary code text still not indexed here.

Carbon, GWP and EPDs

Type IL collection. These points are not general rules for every cement or concrete.

  • About 8% lower GWP: Industry EPDs: PLC (10.8% limestone) is 8.2% lower than portland cement; FHWA cites 8.3%; ACI up to 10%.
  • Grinding: Extra grinding energy is outweighed by clinker reduction.
  • New PCR (Oct 2026): ACA is surveying U.S. producers for updated industry-average EPDs, including portland-limestone cement.
  • Plant-specific Type IL EPDs keep appearing, e.g. Amrize Devils Slide IL(10), issued Feb 2026 (R68).
  • NRMCA released its Industry-Wide LCA and EPD Version 4 in October 2026 (R99, R100), with PLC included in the modeled cement supply.
  • Low-dosage (0.3–0.6%) colloidal nanosilica can boost CO2 uptake in Type IL paste; OPC still carbonates more readily, while nanosilica aids PLC via air stabilization and available CH (R127).
  • ACA blended-cements hub (R138): PLC use since 2021 credited with avoiding over 13.3 Mt CO2; industry-average PLC ~8.2% lower CO2 vs PC per EPDs.
  • CP Tech/FHWA paving carbon interim guide (R137): Type IL typically ~8–10% lower cradle-to-gate GWP if cement content is not increased; do not raise cementitious content to compensate.

Fresh properties and admixtures

Type IL collection. These points are not general rules for every cement or concrete.

  • Higher fineness: Can raise water demand about 1-3 gal/yd3 and reduce bleeding.
  • Admixtures: Re-tune dosages; avoid over-dosing retarding water reducers; verify air entrainment per source.
  • Setting: Reports of unpredictable or differential set and top-down crusting in flatwork.
  • New ASCE JMCE study (R94) of Texas, Colorado and Florida IL cements: lower strength development than Type I/II, higher shrinkage in the first 24 h, but up to 35% higher surface and bulk resistivity; results depended on IL fineness, oxide composition and admixture dosage.
  • FHWA R125: PLC fineness (PSD more than Blaine) and sulfate balance drive early workability and set; ACI R126 shows set accelerators can support high-early-strength PLC mortars comparable to same-clinker OPC within 24 h.
  • NCC/TTCC (R136): Braun Intertec testing 10 PLCs (5 companies, 2 plants) for mortar fresh properties (water demand, bleed, set, stiffening) with SCM and WR sensitivity; Fall 2026 NCC presentation planned — complements FHWA-HRT-25-116 (R125).

Durability

Type IL collection. These points are not general rules for every cement or concrete.

  • Sulfate and thaumasite: PLC alone is more susceptible to thaumasite attack at low temperature; SCMs at sulfate-resisting levels mitigate. Use IL(MS)/IL(HS) or C1012 testing.
  • Freeze-thaw and scaling: Air, low w/cm, finishing and curing govern; scaling of IL concrete is debated (ACI Point of View).
  • ASR, chloride: Handled with SCMs as for portland cement; Purdue found PLC-only chloride diffusion up to 30% higher, much lower with fly ash.
  • Abrasion and carbonation: 2026 Temple study: comparable abrasion when properly proportioned and finished.
  • Sulfate designation changed in C595-26 / M 240-26: Type IL (MS)/(HS) now based mainly on ASTM C452 14-day expansion (MS at 0.050% max) rather than C1012 (Giannini, CI Oct 2026).
  • 2026 papers add caution for aggressive exposures: PLC paste lost far more mass than OPC in citric acid (R66), limestone concrete deteriorated most under combined sulfate and chloride attack while fly ash held up (R67), and a critical review argues durability, not clinker cut alone, should decide whether limestone-rich binders are truly low-carbon (R65).
  • A 2026 Revista ALCONPAT review (R101) of nearly five decades of literature finds porosity, chloride diffusion and carbonation rise with limestone content, mitigated by lower w/c and SCMs.
  • Nanosilica-modified Type IL paste microstructure and carbonation behavior are addressed in ASCE JMCE 2026 (R127); exposure-specific testing remains important.

Field issues and finishing

Type IL collection. These points are not general rules for every cement or concrete.

  • Bleed and finish: Type IL often bleeds slower; premature finish and water on the surface raise blistering/delamination risk (ASCC Field Guide R10; Euclid contractor guide R133).
  • WDM forensic (R135): West Des Moines Type IL pavements with scaling/low strength — nothing wrong with Type IL itself; distress tied to high/variable air, mixing, late retempering, finishing and curing responding to different bleed/set. Microcracking and the interfacial transition zone

Type IL collection. These points are not general rules for every cement or concrete.

  • The ITZ (about 10-50 µm around aggregate) is porous and rich in calcium hydroxide, and microcracks start there first (Mehta & Monteiro; Girboveanu 2021).
  • Drying shrinkage of the paste is restrained by stiff aggregate, which causes microcracks around aggregates. Crack amount depends on aggregate size, volume fraction and stiffness mismatch (Grassl 2010; Li 2020; Bisschop 2002).
  • Type IL / PLC: the Purdue JTRP 2025 report found PLC concretes form thinner ITZs than OPC (filler effect), but water reducers raised ITZ porosity and CH build-up in one PLC, weakening it.
  • Limestone filler: about 5% densifies the ITZ while 10% or more can raise ITZ porosity, and calcite collects at the interface (Ghent; Kotlanova 2021/2022). Limestone surfaces nucleate C-S-H well (Ouyang 2017).
  • Limestone aggregate can bond chemically with paste at later ages (Mehta & Monteiro). Larger limestone grains showed fewer ITZ microcracks (Golewski 2025).
  • Methods: micro-CT/3D imaging (Mac 2021), acoustic emission separates aggregate cracks from paste/ITZ cracks (Farnam 2015), DIC at the ITZ (Zhang 2023), fluorescence microscopy for freeze-thaw (Li 2015).
  • R127 examines Type IL paste microstructure under carbonation with colloidal nanosilica, but still does not provide direct ITZ microcrack imaging comparing commercial Type IL vs OPC concrete.

Mass concrete and thermal control

Type IL collection. These points are not general rules for every cement or concrete.

  • FDOT is the clearest Type IL source: Section 346 requires Type IL, IT or II(MH) for mass elements (180°F core max, 35°F differential), and Materials Manual 9.4 Appendix C publishes adiabatic rise tables for Type IL (about 95°F rise at 10 days).
  • Type IL is not automatically low-heat: finer IL releases more heat in the first 24 h, then less; total heat at 7 days is about 6% lower when dilution dominates (GDOT/Georgia Tech R64; PCA SN3148 R1; PCI Journal R31).
  • ACI 301 limits mass concrete to 160°F maximum (to avoid DEF) and a 35°F core-to-surface differential.
  • ACI 207 guides do not address Type IL specifically.
  • ACI temperature-rise curves trace back to the 1936 Boulder Canyon data; USBR (2015) says correct them for modern cement fineness.
  • DEF with limestone: carbonate can accelerate DEF after high-temperature curing in lab studies, and fly ash suppressed it (Asamoto 2017); none use commercial Type IL.
  • A 2026 PINN surrogate (R128) reconstructs full PLC hydration-heat curves Q(t,α) for 0–40% limestone mixtures and can support thermal screening, but it is modeling—not a Type IL field mass-pour case with temperature monitoring.

Key issues

Points that name Type IL, PLC, or a specific cement type describe that collection. They are not findings about every cement or concrete.

  • Research campaign mode: treat verified additions and candidate-queue leads differently; candidates are not indexed as verified resources until human-quality verification.
  • Scope and evidence quality: binder chemistry, SCM, concrete/application and engineering topic are different axes; abstract/preview/catalogue-level seeds do not establish specification compliance.
  • Durability and carbon tradeoffs: compare functional performance and service life, and verify low-pH steel compatibility and carbonation conditions for alternative binders.
  • Finishing and set in slabs: Industry guidance exists (ASCC R10; Euclid R133) but standards for finishing aids are minimal. WDM forensic (R135) links pavement surface distress to finishing/curing response to slower bleed, not a defective cement.
  • Source-to-source variability: Requalify mixes when cement source changes. FHWA-HRT-25-116 (R125) quantifies commercial PLC composition/reactivity scatter; NCC/Braun Intertec 10-PLC fresh-properties study (R136) due Fall 2026 NCC.
  • Sulfate and low-temperature exposure: Thaumasite risk without adequate SCMs.
  • Deicer scaling: Open debate on IL concrete scaling.
  • C595-26 Type IC and higher limestone IP/IS: Cement type label conveys less about composition.
  • Early strength: IL(HE) acceptance still emerging at DOTs. ACI Materials Journal (R126) finds same-clinker PLC can match OPC <24 h strength in HES mortars with set accelerator; limit SCMs by reactivity when early strength governs.
  • State limestone caps: Some DOTs (Indiana at 10%) cap IL limestone below the 15% C595 maximum, so a national IL product may not qualify everywhere.
  • Aggressive chemical exposure: New 2026 studies show PLC/limestone systems can underperform OPC or fly ash mixes in acid and combined sulfate-chloride exposure, so SCMs and exposure-specific testing matter.
  • ITZ in PLC: PLC tends to form a thinner ITZ, but admixture interactions and filler levels above about 10% can make it more porous. R127 adds Type IL paste microstructure under carbonation with nanosilica; direct imaging of ITZ microcracks in Type IL vs OPC concrete is still scarce.
  • Supply and spec drift: some Pennsylvania producers report Type I/II is more available again (PACA, Oct 2026), while NRMCA's 2026 spec guide urges owners not to mandate a cement type.
  • Mass concrete: Type IL is not automatically low-heat (more heat in the first 24 h), ACI 207 is silent on Type IL, and only Florida DOT addresses Type IL in its mass concrete spec. Keep ACI 301's 160°F max and 35°F differential. PINN heat-curve surrogates (R128) can aid screening but are not a substitute for mix-specific ATR or field monitoring.
  • 2026 standards movement: ASTM C595-26 (published 2026-08-01) adds Type IC and changes Type IL sulfate testing; ACI 332-26 residential code tentatively Nov 2026; ACI 301 revision expected 2027; ACI 323 next edition and ACI 201 PLC durability report in development (NRMCA Fall 2026 convention report, R204). Verify adoption status before specifying.

Research gaps

  • Pass 2 (2026-11-09) is a second batch, not complete coverage. Many pass-2 records are DOI/abstract-level (closed full text); access level is recorded per record in research/portable/verified-sources.jsonl. Still open: China GB 175 / India IS standards, ACI 544/237R/303R/207 texts, USACE/USBR RCC dam manuals, ASTM C91/C270/C618 current-edition reviews, refractory castables practice.
  • Deep pass 1 is a first substantial batch, not complete coverage of any topic or of the internet. 585 unique OpenAlex/Crossref hits remain in the candidate queue for triage.
  • Many new verified records are catalogue/DOI/preview-level: full normative ASTM/API/EN texts and several review PDFs were not obtained. Unknown dates remain blank.
  • New scope is foundational, not complete: 38 additions do not provide comprehensive coverage of all binders, concrete families or jurisdictions.
  • Verification boundary: new PDF text was inspected; web records, abstracts, manufacturer descriptions and standard catalogue scopes are labeled. Original R1–R139 were preserved, not all reverified this pass; R118 authors/year remain unconfirmed.
  • Normative access: EN 197-1 and EN 197-5 official catalogue scopes now added (R149–R150), but full official texts still not reviewed. ACI 318/301 primary code coverage remains open. ACI 225R-16 and 548.6R-19 are dated previews, not asserted latest editions.
  • High priority gaps: dedicated emerging-SCM qualifications; structural CAC/CSA and geopolymer field approvals; API/ISO current well-cement requirements; carbon-cured/CO2-injected concrete field evidence; AAC panels; dry shotcrete; polymer-modified and refractory practice; ECC and non-synthetic FRC design; architectural/exposed-aggregate guidance; creep and service-life models.
  • Commercial/regional gaps: no current regional cement/concrete market cost series, comprehensive local availability inventory or global standards equivalence map. Vendor/project carbon and cost claims are not independent EPDs or market observations.
  • Historical gap statements below record what earlier searches had not found, not proof that no such literature or DOT provision exists. Earlier Type IL thermal/finishing rules and single-study conclusions must not be generalized to all cement/concrete families.
  • Field performance data: Lab-to-field correlation and performance-based specs still developing; WDM forensic (R135) is a strong local case that attributes distress to construction response, not Type IL chemistry.
  • Partly covered now: Shotcrete (one 2026 ACI study) and 3D printing (three lab studies including Nature Comm R132 with CNF+LF).
  • Partly covered now: SCC (one 2018 dissertation). Mass concrete topic expanded (R105-R124) plus PINN hydration-heat surrogate (R128).
  • Partly covered: cost — R132 TEA quotes PLC ~$0.16/kg and limestone ~$0.038/kg (2025 e-commerce) for printable mortar; still no reliable Type IL ready-mix market cost series. Primary ACI 318/301 code text and EN 197-1 official text still not found (ACA hub R138 confirms code inclusion secondarily).
  • Microcracking: R127 addresses Type IL paste microstructure/carbonation with nanosilica, but no direct study yet images ITZ microcracks (SEM, CT, AE or DIC) in commercial Type IL vs OPC concrete, separates limestone aggregate from limestone filler effects in PLC, or applies DIC to PLC. No NIST or FHWA report specific to ITZ cracking in PLC was found.
  • Mass concrete: no field case study of a Type IL mass pour with temperature monitoring; PINN (R128) is modeling/screening only; no same-clinker adiabatic IL vs I/II comparison other than FDOT; DEF with PLC only in lab studies; no DOT other than Florida addresses Type IL in its mass spec; ACI 207 is silent on Type IL.
