Microcracking and the interfacial transition zone

18 resources · Last updated October 30, 2026

Key points

  • The ITZ (about 10-50 µm around aggregate) is porous and rich in calcium hydroxide, and microcracks start there first (Mehta & Monteiro; Girboveanu 2021).
  • Drying shrinkage of the paste is restrained by stiff aggregate, which causes microcracks around aggregates. Crack amount depends on aggregate size, volume fraction and stiffness mismatch (Grassl 2010; Li 2020; Bisschop 2002).
  • Type IL / PLC: the Purdue JTRP 2025 report found PLC concretes form thinner ITZs than OPC (filler effect), but water reducers raised ITZ porosity and CH build-up in one PLC, weakening it.
  • Limestone filler: about 5% densifies the ITZ while 10% or more can raise ITZ porosity, and calcite collects at the interface (Ghent; Kotlanova 2021/2022). Limestone surfaces nucleate C-S-H well (Ouyang 2017).
  • Limestone aggregate can bond chemically with paste at later ages (Mehta & Monteiro). Larger limestone grains showed fewer ITZ microcracks (Golewski 2025).
  • Methods: micro-CT/3D imaging (Mac 2021), acoustic emission separates aggregate cracks from paste/ITZ cracks (Farnam 2015), DIC at the ITZ (Zhang 2023), fluorescence microscopy for freeze-thaw (Li 2015).
