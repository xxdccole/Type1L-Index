Key points

Type IL collection. These points describe portland-limestone (Type IL) mixtures and exposures already indexed. They are not general rules for every cement or concrete.

  • Sulfate and thaumasite: PLC alone is more susceptible to thaumasite attack at low temperature; SCMs at sulfate-resisting levels mitigate. Use IL(MS)/IL(HS) or C1012 testing.
  • Freeze-thaw and scaling: Air, low w/cm, finishing and curing govern; scaling of IL concrete is debated (ACI Point of View).
  • ASR, chloride: Handled with SCMs as for portland cement; Purdue found PLC-only chloride diffusion up to 30% higher, much lower with fly ash.
  • Abrasion and carbonation: 2026 Temple study: comparable abrasion when properly proportioned and finished.
  • Sulfate designation changed in C595-26 / M 240-26: Type IL (MS)/(HS) now based mainly on ASTM C452 14-day expansion (MS at 0.050% max) rather than C1012 (Giannini, CI Oct 2026).
  • 2026 papers add caution for aggressive exposures: PLC paste lost far more mass than OPC in citric acid (R66), limestone concrete deteriorated most under combined sulfate and chloride attack while fly ash held up (R67), and a critical review argues durability, not clinker cut alone, should decide whether limestone-rich binders are truly low-carbon (R65).
  • A 2026 Revista ALCONPAT review (R101) of nearly five decades of literature finds porosity, chloride diffusion and carbonation rise with limestone content, mitigated by lower w/c and SCMs.
  • Nanosilica-modified Type IL paste microstructure and carbonation behavior are addressed in ASCE JMCE 2026 (R127); exposure-specific testing remains important.
