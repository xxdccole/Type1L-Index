Estimate only. Not a substitute for testing, for calorimetry or thermogravimetry, or for thermodynamic modelling (GEMS with Cemdata18). It uses idealised end-state reactions, a Bogue calculation that is known to misstate real phase contents, and a fixed reaction order. Use it for orientation and for comparing mixes, not for specification, compliance or structural decisions.

Batch and hydration

How it calculates

  1. Phases. Modified Bogue per ASTM C150 as given in R880 (Eqs. 5–8): C3S = 4.071C − 7.600S − 6.718A − 1.430F − 2.852SO3 − 5.188CO2; C2S = 2.867S − 0.7544·C3S; C3A = 2.650A − 1.692F; C4AF = 3.043F. Free CaO is subtracted from CaO first when the box is ticked, as Lerch & Bogue did (R210). Simplification: the alternative form for A/F below 0.64 is not implemented; the calculator warns instead. Limestone is CO2 × 100.09/44.01.
  2. Degree of hydration. Either one α for every phase, or per-phase α = 1 − exp(−a(t − b)^c) with Tennis & Jennings' constants (R3883). Simplification: those constants are for one cement at room temperature.
  3. Reactions and order. Silicates: RX01–RX02 with C-S-H C1.7SH4 by default. Sulfate goes to C3A then C4AF as ettringite (RX03, RX07). Leftover aluminate converts ettringite to monosulfate (RX04, RX08). When the limestone that reacts is available, monocarbonate forms instead (RX15). Beyond that it forms hydrogarnet (RX05, RX09). Free lime and periclase follow RX12–RX13. SCMs then consume CH: silica to C1.1SH3.9 (RX17), alumina to strätlingite or C4AH13 (RX18, RX20), slag per the Bentz scheme (RX22). Simplification: the order is fixed and every reaction runs to the stated degree; real systems form mixed and partly crystalline phases.
  4. CH balance. CH from cement minus CH demanded by SCMs. If demand exceeds supply, the SCM reactions are scaled down and a warning is shown.
  5. Water. Bound water is the stoichiometric water in the saturated hydrates as written; free water is the batch water minus that. Powers' 0.23 g/g non-evaporable water is shown for comparison (NEW-BENTZ-2009).
  6. Heat. Q∞ = Σ (phase fraction × heat of complete hydration): C3S 120, β-C2S 62, C3A 207, C4AF 100 cal/g; 149 cal/g SO3 for sulfoaluminate; CaO 278.6 and MgO 203 cal/g (R210); × 4.184 J/cal. SCM heats are per gram reacted: silica fume 780 J/g, slag 800 J/g (NEW-BENTZ-2005); others have no sourced default. Q(α) = Σ (fraction × heat × α of that phase).
  7. Chemical shrinkage. Σ V(reactants incl. water) − Σ V(products), with molar volumes from R334 and NEW-BENTZ-2005. Phases without a sourced volume are left out on both sides.
  8. Powers porosity. p = (w/c)/(w/c + 1/3.15); capillary water = p − 1.32(1−p)α; chemical shrinkage = 0.20(1−p)α; gel water = 0.60(1−p)α; gel solids = 1.52(1−p)α; unreacted cement = (1−p)(1−α). SCMs and limestone are treated as inert filler that scales these fractions by (1 − V_filler) (NEW-BENTZ-2009). Simplification: SCM reaction products are not included in the Powers volumes.

Checks against published values

The script tests/calculator.test.js (run with Node) checks the code against: the Bogue phases of the R1882 cement (within 0.05 %); the Bogue phases and computed heats of Lerch & Bogue's clinkers 4, 5, 15 and 17 (R210, within 1.5 % and 1 cal/g); and Powers results from NEW-BENTZ-2009 (zero porosity at w/c 0.356, 8.7 % capillary porosity at w/c 0.42 sealed, and zero-porosity w/cm for 5–25 % limestone). CH contents against R1882 are a plausibility check only.
